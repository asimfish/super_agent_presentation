Paper [P1] presents LatentPlan for the problem of model-based control. Its method is built around an action-conditioned latent transition model.

The paper evaluates LatentPlan on two simulated control benchmarks. Across three seeds, its reported mean return is 4.1 points higher than that of a behavioral-cloning baseline [P1].

The evidence boundary is narrow: only [P1] is supplied. The reported limitations are the absence of a statistical significance test and a real-robot experiment. Thus, the 4.1-point mean-return difference remains descriptive; the evidence does not establish real-robot validation, leading performance against a broader comparison set, or generalization beyond the two simulated benchmarks.
