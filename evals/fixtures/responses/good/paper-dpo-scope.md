DPO makes an RL loop unnecessary for the paper's offline preference-learning formulation; it does not establish that RL can be abandoned for all language-model alignment. Its contribution is a change of variables: under the Bradley-Terry model, the optimal-policy/reference-policy log ratio represents reward, and the prompt-dependent term cancels between two completions. That gives a classification loss without a separate reward model or policy sampling during fine-tuning. Preference collection can still involve sampling and labeling.

The controlled sentiment comparison is informative because it compares reward at a given KL divergence, rather than treating higher reward with unrestricted policy drift as better optimization. The TL;DR comparison starts from the same SFT model, but its GPT-4 win rates are against reference summaries, not direct DPO-versus-PPO victories. Separate head-to-head human evaluation favors DPO at the tested sampling temperatures; a CNN/DailyMail comparison also provides limited evidence under distribution shift. Dialogue evidence is weaker for comparing optimizers: Best of 128 is a rough proxy after the sourced PPO model fails to provide a useful comparison.

My assessment is that this supports DPO as a practical offline baseline. For an interactive target task, I would first compare it with a functioning RL baseline under matched data and evaluation, rather than treat the reparameterization as a deployment result. That comparison is a proposed follow-up. These conclusions refer to the selected evidence from [Rafailov et al., v3, Sections 3-4 and 6-7](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.18290v3).
