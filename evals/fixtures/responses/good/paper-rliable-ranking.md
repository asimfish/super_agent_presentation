A higher aggregate point estimate is evidence about measured performance, but it does not by itself establish a reliable algorithm ranking. The paper changes the evaluation question from “which score is largest?” to “which comparison survives uncertainty and protocol checks?” Its Atari analysis finds unstable conclusions; non-standard protocols explain some apparent gains as well. These are distinct threats: better uncertainty estimation cannot repair an unfair comparator.

Stratified bootstrap resamples runs within each task, while IQM averages the middle half of pooled run/task scores. Together they offer a robust aggregate and an interval, not a replacement for task-level inspection. Performance profiles expose behaviors hidden by the aggregate, including crossing curves. Marginal interval overlap does not establish equivalence; the relevant comparison needs an interval for the score difference.

For our lab, I would match evaluation protocols first, retain per-run/task results, then report IQM with uncertainty and inspect the profiles before claiming broad improvement. The Atari coverage check with ten runs is specific to that experiment; three-run intervals underestimated uncertainty there, and resampling depends on how well the observations represent the distribution. We should choose additional runs for the precision our decision needs, rather than adopt ten as a universal rule. This is an evaluation recommendation, not a new lab result.
